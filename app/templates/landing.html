How it works

  1. {{ icon('clipboard-text') }}

    Describe the idea

    Ten short questions about the task, its output and who checks it. Every option includes a plain explanation.

  2. {{ icon('file-search') }}

    Classify, with sources

    A classifier labels GMP relevance, model type and autonomy. Each judgment cites a passage from a public regulatory text.

  3. {{ icon('scale') }}

    Fixed rules decide

    The outcome comes from rules QA can read line by line, never from the model. Safety checks can only make it stricter.

  4. {{ icon('list-check') }}

    Controls, questions, draft

    You receive the required controls, questions where information is missing, and a draft intake record for QA.

Who it is for

{{ icon('users-group') }}Proposers and AI builders
Check an idea early, see which controls it needs, and know what QA is likely to ask.
{{ icon('user-check') }}QA reviewers
Receive a consistent first read with its sources and a draft record. You confirm or correct it, and you decide.

What it does not do

  • {{ icon('ban') }}No legal advice and no final compliance decision. Every result is a draft, and QA decides.
  • {{ icon('circle-minus') }}It screens proposed uses. It does not assess the validation status of a system already in use.
  • {{ icon('books') }}It knows only the public texts on the Sources page, not your company procedures.
  • {{ icon('eye-off') }}Do not enter confidential data. Your answers may be sent to an external AI service.
{{ icon('shield-check') }}

How it was tested

It was tested on synthetic cases kept apart from development, each with a manually reviewed label, and compared with a simple keyword checklist. The main criterion: it should never give an outcome more permissive than the correct one.
